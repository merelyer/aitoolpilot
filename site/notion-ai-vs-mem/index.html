Notion AI vs Mem: Best AI Notes App in 2025

Imagine this: You’ve just attended a 45-minute client call. You have a messy pile of handwritten notes, a few voice memos, and a half-typed email draft. You need to turn it into a clear action plan, but you’re drowning in information. Sound familiar? The problem isn’t that you don’t have enough tools—it’s that your notes are scattered and your apps don’t think for you.

That’s where AI-powered notes apps come in. They don’t just store text; they summarize, connect, and generate insights. In 2025, two names dominate the conversation: Notion AI and Mem. Both promise to supercharge your note-taking, but they take wildly different approaches. In this head-to-head comparison, I’ll break down their features, pricing, strengths, and weaknesses so you can decide which one truly deserves a spot in your daily workflow.

TL;DR — Our #1 Pick

If you want a versatile workspace that combines notes, databases, and AI assistance, Notion AI is our top pick for most users. It’s more mature, integrates with your existing Notion setup, and offers a generous free plan. However, if you crave an automatic, self-organizing knowledge base that connects your notes without manual tagging, Mem is a close contender—especially for students and researchers who live in their notes.

Deep Dive: Notion AI

Notion has been a productivity powerhouse since 2016, and its AI features (launched in 2023) have only solidified its position. Notion AI isn’t a separate app—it’s an integrated layer that works across your pages, databases, and documents. You can ask it to draft content, summarize meeting notes, translate text, or even generate tables from raw data.

Key Features

  • Context-aware writing: Notion AI understands your entire workspace, so it can answer questions like “What were the action items from last week’s design review?” using your existing notes.
  • Autofill and database automation: Generate properties for database entries, such as summaries or tags, saving hours of manual data entry.
  • Meeting notes and action items: Paste a transcript, and Notion AI will extract key decisions and next steps.
  • Translation and tone adjustment: Rewrite notes in a formal, casual, or friendly tone—perfect for client-facing emails.
  • Slash commands: Type /ai to instantly access AI features without breaking your flow.

Pricing

Notion’s base plans are free for personal use, but AI is a paid add-on. Here’s the breakdown:

  • Free Plan: $0 (includes basic note-taking, but no AI access)
  • AI Add-on: $10/month per member (when billed annually) or $12 monthly
  • Business Plan: $20/month per member (includes AI and advanced admin tools)

Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Deep integration with Notion’s powerful database and wiki features
  • Excellent for team collaboration—share AI-generated content in real-time
  • Generous free tier for non-AI features
  • Works offline (AI features require internet, but notes are accessible offline)

Cons:

  • AI is not included in the free plan—you must pay extra
  • Can be overwhelming for new users due to the sheer number of features
  • No automatic relationship-building between notes (unlike Mem)

Deep Dive: Mem

Mem is a relatively newer player, founded in 2020, but it has quickly gained a cult following. Its core philosophy is “notes that think for you.” Instead of manually organizing notes into folders, Mem uses AI to automatically link related ideas, surface relevant notes, and even generate a daily digest of important information. It’s like having a personal assistant that knows everything you’ve ever written.

Key Features

  • Automatic bi-directional linking: Mem creates connections between notes based on content similarity, so you don’t have to manually tag or link.
  • AI-powered search: Search isn’t just keyword-based; Mem understands context and can answer questions like “What did I learn about machine learning last month?”
  • Daily digest: Each morning, Mem compiles a summary of your recent notes, tasks, and calendar events—so you start your day with context.
  • Voice capture: Record voice memos on the go, and Mem transcribes and organizes them automatically.
  • Note generation from templates: Use built-in templates for meeting notes, journaling, or research, and Mem will format them with AI assistance.

Pricing

Mem offers a freemium model, but the AI features are heavily gated:

  • Free Plan: $0 (unlimited notes, but limited AI queries and no auto-linking)
  • Pro Plan: $8.33/month (billed annually) or $10 monthly (includes full AI, auto-linking, and daily digest)
  • Team Plan: $15/month per user (adds collaboration features)

Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Truly automatic organization—no manual folders or tags
  • Excellent for capturing fleeting thoughts via voice or quick notes
  • AI feels more “alive” because it proactively surfaces related notes
  • Clean, distraction-free interface

Cons:

  • Limited third-party integrations compared to Notion
  • Free plan is very restrictive—you’ll likely need to upgrade to Pro
  • No offline mode (requires internet connection)

Who Should Use What

Choose Notion AI if:

  • You’re already using Notion for project management, wikis, or databases.
  • You work in a team that needs shared AI-assisted documents.
  • You want a single tool that replaces multiple apps (notes, tasks, docs).
  • You need offline access to your notes.

Choose Mem if:

  • You’re a student or researcher who takes massive amounts of notes and wants them auto-organized.
  • You prefer a zero-maintenance system—no folder creation or tagging.
  • You rely heavily on voice memos and quick captures.
  • You want a daily digest of your notes and tasks without manual curation.

Bottom Line Verdict

After testing both extensively, I can say that Notion AI is the more robust, feature-complete solution—especially if you’re building a productivity system that includes tasks, databases, and collaboration. Its AI is a powerful assistant that works within your existing structure, and the free tier is generous enough for casual users.

However, Mem is a game-changer for note-heavy individuals who hate organizing. Its automatic linking and daily digest feel like magic, and the AI search is genuinely impressive. The trade-off is a steeper learning curve for the AI’s limitations and a less integrated ecosystem.

My recommendation: If you’re starting from scratch and want a notes app that grows with you, go with Notion AI. If you already have a mountain of notes and want them to become a connected knowledge base without lifting a finger, try Mem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Notion AI worth the extra cost?

Yes, if you use Notion regularly. The AI can save hours by drafting summaries, generating content, and automating database entries. For heavy users, the $10/month add-on pays for itself in productivity gains.

Does Mem have a free plan?

Yes, Mem offers a free plan with unlimited notes, but AI features like auto-linking and daily digest are limited. You’ll need the Pro plan ($8.33/month) to unlock the full AI experience.

Which app is better for students?

Mem is often better for students because it automatically connects lecture notes, readings, and ideas. However, Notion AI is better if you need to structure research projects with databases and collaborate with classmates.

Can Notion AI replace Mem?

Not entirely. Notion AI can generate and summarize content, but it doesn’t automatically link related notes across your workspace the way Mem does. If auto-organization is your priority, Mem has the edge.

Do these apps work offline?

Notion allows offline access to your notes (but AI features require internet). Mem is online-only, so you need a connection to access your notes and AI features.

Which app has better privacy?

Both have strong security measures. Notion offers team-level permissions and 2FA. Mem encrypts data in transit and at rest, but it’s a smaller company, so some enterprises may prefer Notion’s compliance certifications.

Ready to Boost Your Productivity?

Both tools offer free trials, so you can test them risk-free. I highly recommend starting with Notion AI—it’s the safest bet for most users. But if you’re curious about Mem, try Mem and see how its auto-organization feels. Your brain will thank you.
